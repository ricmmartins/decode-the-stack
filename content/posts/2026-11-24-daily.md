### [JEP 545 Moves ZGC Memory Preparation off the Startup Path in JDK 28](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/11/jep-545-jdk-28/?utm_campaign=infoq_content&utm_source=infoq&utm_medium=feed&utm_term=global)
*ZGC’s startup improvements come with an important configuration qualification.*

InfoQ reports that JEP 545 has been integrated into JDK 28, moving heap-memory preparation onto concurrent worker threads. ZGC now pre-touches memory as the heap expands and upgrades small pages to large ones on Linux 6.1 and later. This moves preparation work away from the startup path rather than merely presenting a new tuning recommendation.

The reported 38% startup and 90% warmup gains require careful interpretation. According to the supplied account, the 2 MB initial heap behind those results depends on JEP 546's adaptive heap-sizing flag, which is disabled by default. Teams should therefore distinguish the integrated ZGC changes from the particular configuration used for the headline results; neither percentage is established here as an automatic benefit for existing deployments.

*A PostgreSQL incident account shows why connection limits cannot be sized independently of query memory and parallelism.*

The author reports that a 16 GB PostgreSQL 17 primary received a kernel SIGKILL during a payroll batch after a configuration combining `max_connections=500` and `work_mem=64MB` encountered parallel hash joins. The account says analytical queries used four parallel workers and three hash-and-sort nodes, with 65 active sessions allocating more than 15 GB of private heap. The issue is the interaction between concurrency and memory-consuming execution operations, not the connection setting in isolation.

The article recommends PgBouncer and a `shared_buffers` starting point of 25% of RAM, while promoting a sizing calculator and a lower-cost hosting target. Those are author recommendations and a proposed target, not demonstrated universal settings or equivalent-service savings. Teams should budget for concurrent query execution and validate workload assumptions before treating a connection ceiling or calculator-generated configuration as safe.
