*A release gate’s response to missing evidence is a policy decision, not merely an error-handling detail.*

The author describes a familiar pipeline dilemma: fail open and release despite an unobserved gap, or fail closed and risk stopping delivery because a tool could not complete its work. Their gate withholds its score when coverage is partial, emitting `WITHHELD` rather than a numeric result. The rationale is that a score based on incomplete observation can look authoritative and be reused as though the evidence were complete.

A negative finding and an unavailable assessment are different states. Treating both as an ordinary number collapses materially different states; treating every unavailable assessment as a hard stop carries an availability cost. The article does not establish one universal stopping rule. Its central operational argument is to make each check’s incomplete-evidence behavior explicit, so downstream decisions do not mistake uncertainty for confidence.
