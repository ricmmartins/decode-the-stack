### [Factory Method and Simple Factory (Design Patterns)](https://dev.to/divakarupadhyay/factory-method-and-simple-factory-design-patterns-don)
*Object-creation patterns matter most when they keep implementation choices out of calling code.*

The supplied lesson describes factory-based creation through a simple contract: a method determines which concrete class to instantiate, while callers depend on an abstraction. Its C# example introduces an `INotification` interface with a `Send` operation. The architectural question is which part of the application should know the object's concrete type, as well as how to construct it.

That separation can make implementation choices easier to change without spreading construction decisions through business logic. It also introduces indirection, so the value depends on whether creation actually needs a boundary. The excerpt does not contain the lesson's full comparison of Factory Method and Simple Factory; it supports the shared motivation, not a detailed assessment of the two approaches.
